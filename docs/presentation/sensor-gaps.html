HiTide · data coverage report · Texas

Where the water rises, the sensors aren't there

Every public flood feed we use is real and useful. Each one also misses the place a driver actually stops: the low-water crossing on a small creek. These numbers come from the USGS site registry, the Census county file, NEXRAD geometry and our own Kerr County road model.

Exhibit 1 · statewide

A quarter of Texas has no river gauge

USGS runs 777 real-time stream-stage gauges in Texas. Spread evenly, that is one per 336 square miles. They are not spread evenly: they cluster in Houston, Austin, San Antonio and Dallas. 64 counties have none and 55 more have exactly one, so 47% of Texas counties rely on one gauge or zero.

Exhibit 2 · what gauges watch

Gauges sit on rivers. Roads cross creeks.

Gauges are built to measure big rivers. The median Texas gauge measures a basin of 297 square miles. Only 33 of the 671 gauges with a recorded basin (5%) measure a creek draining under 10 square miles. Most of the crossings in our region sit on creeks that small.

Exhibit 4 · our demo region

63 of 98 critical crossings have no gauge on their creek

Our road model of Hunt, Ingram and Kerrville finds 98 bridges, low-water crossings and low spots that can cut people off. Five USGS gauges reported on July 4, 2025. They sit on the Guadalupe, its North Fork and Johnson Creek. Quinlan, Town, Third, Camp Meeting and Cypress Creeks and the South Fork have none, and those creeks carry 64% of the crossings. The median crossing is 3.5 km from the nearest gauge, and 10 are more than 5 km from one.

Exhibit 5 · radar

Radar sees rain a mile up, not water on the road

The nearest NWS radar (KEWX, New Braunfels) is 114 km from Kerrville and 133 km from Hunt. The Earth curves away beneath its beam, so even the lowest 0.5° scan passes 1.5 km above Kerrville and 1.9 km above Hunt, and by then the beam is 1.9 km wide. Radar estimates how much rain is falling. It cannot tell whether a specific crossing is under water.

Exhibit 6 · July 4, 2025

The gauge that mattered most went dark at the crest

The Guadalupe River gauge at Hunt is the first warning for everyone downstream. On July 4, 2025 it rose past major flood stage, logged 37.52 ft at 5:10 a.m. CDT and then stopped reporting for 34 h 25 min. Its true crest was never recorded, and everything downstream ran without its readings through the worst hours.

Sources and method

Gauges: USGS Water Services site registry, Texas, stream sites with active instantaneous gage-height data (parameter 00065), pulled 2026-11-08. Counties and land areas: U.S. Census 2024 Gazetteer. Radar: KEWX location and standard 4/3-Earth beam geometry; ground heights from the USGS 3DEP 30 m model. Region: OpenStreetMap roads and waterways for Kerr County, critical-crossing model in this project. July 4 hydrograph: USGS Water Data OGC API, approved values.
